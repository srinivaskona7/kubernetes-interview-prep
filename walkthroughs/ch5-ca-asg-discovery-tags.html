How does the Cluster Autoscaler know which Auto Scaling groups it may manage on EKS?

Chapter 5 mid walkthrough · 4 steps
Say first

Through two tags on the Auto Scaling group itself: one that opts the group in, and one that ties it to a specific cluster name.

⚠ Predicted failure

  • Pitfall: Tagging instances but not the ASG.
  • Pitfall: Misspelling the cluster name inside the tag key.
  • If ignored, this surfaces as: Unknown.

✓ Best troubleshooting path

  • Reproduce with the diagnostic command (step 1-2).
  • The CA does not grab every node group in your account. Think of two sticky notes on a machine: one says 'available for autoscaling', the other says 'belongs to cluster X'. Without both notes, the CA walks straight past.
  • The tags must be on the ASG, not only on the EC2 instances it creates. With eksctl you declare them in the managed node group config and eksctl copies them onto the ASG.
  • Also set sane minSize and maxSize. They are your safety rails: a floor for baseline capacity and a ceiling so a runaway workload cannot launch unlimited machines and bills.
  • Apply the fix command (step 3), then verify (step 4).
  • Confirm status becomes Healthy.

Explain

  • The CA does not grab every node group in your account. Think of two sticky notes on a machine: one says 'available for autoscaling', the other says 'belongs to cluster X'. Without both notes, the CA walks straight past.
  • The tags must be on the ASG, not only on the EC2 instances it creates. With eksctl you declare them in the managed node group config and eksctl copies them onto the ASG.
  • Also set sane minSize and maxSize. They are your safety rails: a floor for baseline capacity and a ceiling so a runaway workload cannot launch unlimited machines and bills.

Key points

  • Both tags are mandatory for auto-discovery.
  • Tags belong on the ASG; instance-only tags are not enough.
  • The cluster-specific tag stops one CA managing another cluster's nodes.
  • minSize and maxSize act as cost and availability guardrails.
