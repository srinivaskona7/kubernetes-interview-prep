When would you choose topology spread constraints over Pod anti-affinity?

Chapter 13 senior walkthrough · 4 steps
Say first

Choose topology spread for even, tunable distribution of many replicas across zones and nodes; choose anti-affinity for a strict 'never together' rule.

⚠ Predicted failure

  • Pitfall: Claiming spread constraints replace anti-affinity in every case.
  • If ignored, this surfaces as: NotReady.

✓ Best troubleshooting path

  • Reproduce with the diagnostic command (step 1-2).
  • Anti-affinity is binary. It can say 'no two of these on one node', but it cannot say 'balance eight replicas evenly across three zones'. Once replicas outnumber domains, the hard version simply fails.
  • Spread constraints express a tolerance. Allow a skew of 1 and the scheduler fills domains in turn, so adding replicas just keeps working. You can also choose hard or soft, and combine zone and node levels.
  • Anti-affinity is still the right tool for a small set of Pods that must never share a node, such as the members of a quorum-based database. For stateless replicated services, spread constraints are now the usual first choice.
  • Apply the fix command (step 3), then verify (step 4).
  • Confirm status becomes Ready.

Explain

  • Anti-affinity is binary. It can say 'no two of these on one node', but it cannot say 'balance eight replicas evenly across three zones'. Once replicas outnumber domains, the hard version simply fails.
  • Spread constraints express a tolerance. Allow a skew of 1 and the scheduler fills domains in turn, so adding replicas just keeps working. You can also choose hard or soft, and combine zone and node levels.
  • Anti-affinity is still the right tool for a small set of Pods that must never share a node, such as the members of a quorum-based database. For stateless replicated services, spread constraints are now the usual first choice.

Key points

  • Spread = balance with a tolerance; anti-affinity = exclusion.
  • Spread scales smoothly past the number of domains; required anti-affinity does not.
  • Both use labelSelector and topologyKey.
  • They can be used together.
  • Neither moves Pods that are already running.
