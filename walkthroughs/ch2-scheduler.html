How does the scheduler decide where a Pod runs?

Chapter 2 mid walkthrough · 4 steps
Say first

It filters out nodes that cannot host the Pod, scores the remaining ones, and binds the Pod to the best scorer, considering resource requests, affinity, taints and tolerations, and spreading rules.

Explain

  • Think of a matchmaker. First rule out the impossible (not enough CPU, wrong labels, tainted node). Then rank what is left (spread, locality, free capacity) and pick the winner.
  • The scheduler uses the Pod's resource requests, not live usage, to judge fit. That is why setting requests correctly matters.
  • All the knobs are set by whoever writes the manifest: nodeSelector, affinity and anti-affinity, tolerations, topology spread constraints and priority.

Key points

  • Two phases: filter (feasibility) then score (preference).
  • Decisions use requests, not limits or current usage.
  • Affinity attracts Pods; anti-affinity and spread keep replicas apart for availability.
  • A Pod nobody fits stays Pending with an explanatory event.
